The story behind the name

Make it. Copy it. Pass it on.

Samizdat is a desktop studio for making small, physical publications. The name comes from a history of people publishing outside official channels—with modest tools, shared effort, and a reader who became the next distributor.

The short version

In Russian, samizdat means roughly “self-published” or “published by oneself.” It came to describe writing copied and circulated outside official Soviet publishing channels.

A name with history: use it with care.Make something small. Put it in someone’s hands.

Why this name?

Not a costume. A practice.

Samizdat referred to texts that were typed, copied, and passed from person to person outside official publishing systems. The material was often modest: paper, carbon copies, a typewriter, a trusted reader, and the willingness to make another copy.

It was not only a look or a genre. It was a way for ideas, testimony, literature, and arguments to circulate when the sanctioned channels would not carry them. The work moved because people made it, copied it, read it, and handed it onward.

For the historical record: the Library of Congress describes samizdat as a Soviet term meaning roughly “privately published,” applied to writing circulated outside official publication channels. Read the Library of Congress overview ↗

The practice

A small publishing system.

The important verbs are ordinary ones: make, reproduce, circulate.

01 / MAKE

Start with what you have.

A typewriter, a printer, a photocopier, a kitchen table, or a computer. The point is to lower the distance between an idea and a thing.

02 / CIRCULATE

Let the work travel.

A publication becomes public through readers: a handoff, a mailbox, a classroom table, a community noticeboard, a friend who makes another copy.

03 / CONTINUE

Keep the channel open.

Samizdat borrows that commitment to accessible making and hand-to-hand circulation—not the claim that a small app recreates the historical risks of dissident publishing.

What the name means here

A tool for publishing outside the feed.

Samizdat is for zines, field guides, classroom projects, neighborhood news, art, invitations, arguments, instructions, and tiny publications that deserve to become physical objects.

It keeps the making local and puts the page-order and folding work in the background. You author upright pages; Samizdat helps turn them into a sheet you can print, cut, fold, and pass along.

We use the name as a reminder that publishing does not have to begin with a platform. Sometimes it begins with a person making a thing for another person.
