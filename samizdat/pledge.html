The Samizdat Pledge

Pass a little on.

Samizdat is a small business making a tool for public expression. A portion of what it earns should help make room for more of that work in the world.

The direct route is always open

If an organization’s work matters to you, give to it directly. The Pledge is an additional commitment from Samizdat—not a substitute for your support.

Current recipient: Campaign for Criminal Justice and TransparencyKalamazoo, Michigan

How it works

Giving belongs in the design.

A good pledge should be easy to understand, easy to verify, and paid on a schedule.

01 / DEFINE IT

Name the basis.

The current Samizdat pledge is 3% of Samizdat and affiliate proceeds, passed on annually. Before launch, we will publish the final accounting basis and payout date plainly.

02 / CHOOSE WELL

Follow the alignment.

Recipients should expand public knowledge, accountability, participation, or the ability of people to act together—not merely look good beside a product.

03 / SHOW THE WORK

Keep a record.

We will name the recipient, explain the connection, report what was passed on, and update this page when the recipient changes.

Current recipient · Kalamazoo

Campaign for Criminal Justice and Transparency.

The Campaign for Criminal Justice and Transparency collaborates with the entire community to create an anti-oppressive criminal legal system that is accountable and transparent to the public.

Its work is local, practical, and public-facing: building community knowledge, asking institutions to explain themselves, and creating space for people affected by the legal system to participate in the conversation. Current work includes public attention to Kalamazoo County’s jail telecommunications contract and education around “Resisting & Obstructing” charges.

CCJT also made a zine for a recent community-education project around “Resisting & Obstructing” charges. That connection matters to Samizdat: a difficult public issue became something people could read, discuss, carry home, and share.

Why this matters here

Public knowledge is a material.

A zine cannot repair an institution by itself. But it can help a person understand a system, recognize a pattern, find a meeting, ask a better question, or discover that other people are already working on the same problem.

That is the modest theory behind the Pledge: the same spirit that moves a publication from one hand to another can help move attention, resources, and public capacity toward people doing accountable work.
