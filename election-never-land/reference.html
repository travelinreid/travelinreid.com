Research cutoff November 12, 2026. A curated selection, not an exhaustive census.

1824Legislative election

First Hispanic U.S. senator

Charles Dominique Bouligny

Louisiana's legislature elected Bouligny to the Senate in 1824.

The Senate Historical Office revised this attribution in October 2026; older references name Octaviano Larrazolo. Bouligny was born a Spanish citizen in Spanish-ruled New Orleans. When Spanish loyalists were encouraged to relocate after the Louisiana Purchase, Bouligny resigned from the Spanish military and remained in New Orleans.

  • U.S. Senate Historical Office: Charles Dominique Bouligny: First Hispanic Senator
1870Entered office

First black member to serve in the U.S. House

Joseph Rainey

Rainey began House service in December 1870.

Date denotes House service, not Election Day. Revels had already entered the Senate.

  • U.S. House of Representatives: Firsts Among Members
1870Entered office

First black member of Congress

Hiram Revels

Revels took his Senate oath on February 25, 1870.

Revels was elected to complete an existing term ending in March 1871. Opponents challenged his eligibility, citing the Dred Scott decision and the Senate’s nine-year citizenship requirement.

  • U.S. Senate Historical Office: Hiram Revels: First African American Senator
  • U.S. House Office of the Historian: Hiram Rhodes Revels
1876Election

First Hispanic voting representative elected to the U.S. House

Romualdo Pacheco

Pacheco's initial 1876 victory sent him to the House in 1877.

This concerns a voting representative; territorial delegates had served earlier. The House later resolved his contested election against him in 1878; he won again in 1879.

  • U.S. House of Representatives: Election Credentials of Romualdo Pacheco
1881Term completed

First black senator to complete a full term

Blanche Kelso Bruce

Bruce completed his six-year Senate term in 1881, after election by the Mississippi legislature in 1874.

Election: February 4, 1874. Service: March 4, 1875–March 3, 1881. The timeline year is completion, not election. He was the second black senator, after Hiram Revels.

  • U.S. House of Representatives: Blanche Kelso Bruce

Brandeis joined the Court in 1916.

Supreme Court justices are appointed and confirmed, not elected.

  • Supreme Court of the United States: First Jewish U.S. Supreme Court justice
1916Election

First woman elected to Congress

Jeannette Rankin

Following her election, reporters focused on the important issues, asking about her clothes, her recipes, and the color of her hair.

Election year and service year differ. House firsts records her oath/service on April 2, 1917.

  • U.S. House of Representatives: Women in Congress: Fast Facts
  • U.S. House of Representatives: Firsts Among Members
  • U.S. House Office of the Historian: A Womanly Woman with Womanly Ambitions
1922Entered office

First woman to serve in the U.S. Senate

Rebecca Latimer Felton

Though she was a journalist, feminist, and suffragist, Felton was also a white supremacist.

Appointed October 3; sworn in November 21 and replaced November 22. She was not elected.

  • U.S. Senate Historical Office: Rebecca Felton and One Hundred Years of Women Senators
  • U.S. House Office of the Historian: Rebecca Latimer Felton
1925Entered office

First woman to serve as a U.S. state governor

Nellie Tayloe Ross

Ross became Wyoming's governor in 1925 after winning an election to replace her late husband.

Miriam Ferguson also won a governorship in 1924 but took office later in January 1925; this record is the service first.

  • Center for American Women and Politics, Rutgers: History of Women Governors
  • National Governors Association: Nellie Tayloe Ross
1928Election

First Native American elected vice president

Charles Curtis

Though a citizen of the Kaw Nation, Curtis supported policies that undermined tribal sovereignty.

Curtis won the vice presidency with Herbert Hoover in 1928.

1932Election

First woman elected to the U.S. Senate

Hattie Caraway

Known as "Silent Hattie" for only speaking 15 times on the Senate floor, Caraway won a special election in January 1932.

She had already entered the Senate by appointment in November 1931; her service therefore predates her election.

1966Election

First black senator elected by popular vote

Edward Brooke

Brooke won election in 1966 and entered the Senate in 1967, ending a nearly 86-year absence of black senators.

Revels and Bruce had been selected by state legislatures. The 86-year interval runs from Bruce leaving in 1881 to Brooke serving in 1967.

  • U.S. House of Representatives: Edward William Brooke III

1968Election

First black woman elected to Congress

Shirley Chisholm

Chisholm bucked traditional expectations of freshmen representatives to not "rock the boat," becoming an immediately vocal advocate on issues such as the Vietnam War.

Chisholm’s freshman class included two other black members, increasing black representation in the U.S. House from six to nine.

At least 29 states have neither had nor elected a Hispanic U.S. representative

Eighteen states have had Hispanic House members. Nebraska and North Carolina elected their first in 2026; service begins in 2027. New Hampshire requires further review.

New Hampshire is unverified: congressional historical rosters do not list John E. Sununu, whose father, governor John H. Sununu, publicly identified as partly Hispanic. A parent’s identity does not establish how the son identifies.

It is the only state still without this first.

CAWP reported that no women filed for Mississippi’s U.S. House races in 2026. Cindy Hyde-Smith has served in the Senate; this claim concerns the House only.
